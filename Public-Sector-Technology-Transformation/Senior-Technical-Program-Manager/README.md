# Senior Technical Program Manager | Public Sector

## What This Role Looks Like
I would own the connective tissue across product, engineering, security, data, analytics, customer operations, vendors, and executive stakeholders. My responsibility is not simply to maintain a schedule. It is to make sure the program can move from decision to execution without losing time at handoffs.

## Day-to-Day
A typical day would include reviewing milestone health and critical-path dependencies, meeting with technical leads on blockers, validating whether customer decisions are overdue, checking UAT and release-readiness conditions, reconciling vendor and internal delivery commitments, preparing concise leadership updates, and converting new risks into owned recovery actions.

I would regularly move between detailed delivery work and executive context: one hour may be spent resolving an API dependency, while the next is spent explaining to leadership how that dependency affects the release date, customer adoption, or cost.

## Common Challenges in the Role
- Multiple teams are individually busy but the program still loses time between teams.
- Technical dependencies are discovered after dates have already been committed.
- Leadership status is optimistic because each team reports its own work rather than the integrated release condition.
- Decisions sit in email or meetings without owners or due dates.
- UAT begins before data, security, or integration prerequisites are stable.
- A modernization release is treated as successful even if operations cannot sustain it after launch.

## How I Would Solve Them
I would establish an integrated delivery control connecting workstream, milestone, ECD, dependency, decision, owner, risk, fallback, UAT, release readiness, adoption, and recovery action.

My first move would be to reconstruct the real critical path from the operating process rather than accepting the existing project plan at face value. From there, I would identify which dependencies can actually stop delivery, which leadership decisions have deadlines, and which work can continue in parallel.

I would then build a decision and dependency operating cadence: each critical item has an owner, due date, impact, escalation threshold, fallback, and closure evidence. This allows the team to manage what can stop the program rather than merely reporting status.

## Common Solutions I Would Use
Integrated delivery plan • RAID register • dependency map • decision log • release-readiness scorecard • UAT entry/exit criteria • executive operating brief • recovery action plan • milestone forecasting • automated overdue alerts • adoption transition plan.

## Example Scenario
A public-sector technology customer is modernizing a high-volume operating process. Delivery is fragmented across product, security, data, integration, analytics, customer operations, vendors, and leadership. Each team has a tracker, but no one view explains whether the program can deliver the next release.

The program is not failing because teams are inactive. It is losing time at handoffs: access is waiting on security, integration is waiting on source owners, UAT is waiting on a stable build, and leadership decisions are buried in meeting notes.

## Execution Approach
1. Baseline the operating process and identify the actual critical path.
2. Reconcile separate team plans into one dependency model.
3. Establish decision SLAs and accountable escalation owners.
4. Separate delivery status from release readiness.
5. Score technical, data, security, UAT, operational-support, and continuity gates.
6. Automate overdue-decision and dependency alerts.
7. Run structured blocker and recovery reviews.
8. Publish leadership reporting that shows condition, cause, owner, decision, and projected impact.
9. Keep the legacy operating path available until acceptance and rollback requirements are met.
10. Measure adoption and stabilization after release instead of ending the program at go-live.

## What I Would Measure
Milestone attainment • decision aging • blocker aging • dependency closure • UAT pass rate • defect escape • release readiness • adoption • recovery action closure • planned vs actual delivery • time from escalation to decision.

## What Makes My Approach Different
I do not separate the technical plan from the operating result. If a data dependency is late, I want to know which milestone it affects, which customer decision is required, whether another workstream can continue, and what the fallback is. That allows me to lead technical delivery while still speaking in terms of operational impact and executive decision-making.

## Business Continuity
Every critical workstream has a fallback. A modernization release cannot remove the current operating path until acceptance, support, rollback, and continuity criteria are satisfied.
